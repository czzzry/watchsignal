# Product

## Register

product

## Platform

web

## Users

WatchSignal is primarily for Cezary and his wife choosing a movie together at home.
They use one shared phone, usually from the couch, and need a quick way to reach a decision without one person's visible reactions steering the other person's answers.
This is a private household product rather than a public streaming catalogue or a commercial recommendation service.

## Product Purpose

WatchSignal should recommend one movie that both people are genuinely likely to want to watch tonight.
It learns each person's durable taste, applies confirmed needs for the current night, checks practical availability, collects five private reactions from each participating person, and returns one ranked result with four credible alternatives.
Success means the household trusts that the shortlist came from their taste rather than a generic popularity feed, understands when the system cannot produce a real recommendation, and reaches a satisfying choice without doom-scrolling.

## Positioning

WatchSignal is the private movie decision tool that searches from learned personal taste first, protects independent opinions, and ranks the strongest shared fit for tonight.

## Brand Personality

Cinematic, discerning, human.
The product should feel exciting enough to begin movie night, calm enough to support a decision, and plain-spoken enough that its recommendations and failures are easy to trust.
Its voice is concise, specific, and conversational rather than technical, promotional, or algorithmically self-congratulatory.

## Anti-references

WatchSignal must not feel like a generic streaming-service clone, a gamer skin, neon decoration pasted over ordinary app structure, or a flat conservative utility app.
It must not look cheap, sparse without meaning, overcrowded with explanatory copy, or recognizably AI-generated.
Avoid generic recommendation language, internal model terminology, repetitive source labels, decorative dashboards, oversized close-up faces, fake poster art, and hero imagery that overwhelms the product's purpose.
Do not hide a weak or failed recommendation path behind an apparently valid fallback.

## Design Principles

1. Start with taste, not popularity.
Every recommendation surface should reinforce that WatchSignal searches broadly from the active profiles and then refines for the household and tonight.
2. Protect independent opinions.
Private reactions stay sealed through the handoff, and navigation must never reveal another participant's ballot.
3. Make one decision feel special.
Movie imagery and short signature transitions may create anticipation, but every screen should still point to one obvious next action.
4. Explain only what helps.
Use short, human reasons and honest recovery messages while keeping diagnostics, provenance, and scoring detail out of the normal household path.
5. Fail visibly and safely.
If learned retrieval, persistence, provider data, or ranking cannot produce the promised experience, say so clearly and preserve the user's work rather than substituting a misleading fallback.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Body and control text must meet a 4.5 to 1 contrast ratio, and meaningful large graphics must meet a 3 to 1 contrast ratio.
Every interactive control needs a visible two-pixel focus ring with a two-pixel offset and a pointer target of at least 44 by 44 pixels.
Dialogs must use real dialog semantics, trap focus, close through keyboard and visible controls, and return focus to their opener.
The complete flow must support keyboard input, 200 percent text zoom, mobile safe areas, forced colors, reduced motion, reduced transparency, slow or missing images, and long German labels.
Color must never carry state by itself, and status changes should use restrained live-region announcements.
